Where should the suction cup go?

A picking robot looks down into a tote of mixed items and has to choose one point to grab. This page runs the perception model in your browser: it outlines each item and scores every pixel for whether a vacuum cup would seal there and lift the item free. Pick a tote, then check the top choices against the simulator's ground truth.

    Clearing whole totes

    Each policy sees only the camera. On a successful pick the item is lifted out and the scene is re-rendered; a failed point is not retried. Every tote gets two attempts per item, and scoring stops once nothing left in the tote can be lifted (mesh bags and items buried under them go to a person).

    How it is built

    Simulated totes with exact labels

    A procedural RGB-D renderer stacks boxes, envelopes, bottles, cans, pouches, mesh bags and balls. It randomises lighting, shadows, specular glare, sensor noise and depth dropout. A physics oracle labels a pixel pickable only if the cup's whole footprint is flat enough for that material, clear of edges, and on an item with nothing resting on it.

    One network, four maps

    A COCO-pretrained MobileNetV3 LR-ASPP is fine-tuned in PyTorch with a fourth depth input channel. It outputs tote, item, boundary and suction probability. Depth is dropped for 30% of training images, so the same model still works when the depth camera fails.

    From pixels to a pick pose

    OpenCV turns the maps into item instances and suction candidates with non-maximum suppression. A plane fit to the depth patch gives the cup's approach normal. The same model serves through a FastAPI microservice and here through ONNX Runtime Web.
